### Bachelor of Engineering Thesis

## Abstract

The aim of the work is to develop a docking station for drones. The station will be apart of a more extensive early response system to various alarms, reports, and threats. The thesis describes the process of creating the station's mechanical and software parts. In addition, it addresses issues related to mechanics, control of executive elements, and remote monitoring.

The first part of the dissertation contains descriptions of existing solutions to determine their characteristics and compare them to the thesis project. These considerations will help to identify the desired market features and capabilities of the designed system.

The next stage is to introduce the UAV used as a demonstrator. This section includes not only descriptions of modifications that it underwent but also information about its components - it is not a commercial drone but a self-made one.

The following chapter of the thesis is dedicated to the station's construction. First, the program which was used to design was described. Then, various types of possible design solutions were considered to select the optimal one. Necessary calculations were performed. The executive elements (e.g. motors) were chosen for the set construction elements. The process of building the station and the TIG welding technology was presented.

Electronic circuits responsible for controlling the station were selected for the previously chosen executive and construction elements. A comparison of possible solutions was made. The individual circuits were described, and specific ones were selected for each application. Additional components which extend the capabilities of the main control elements were also explained.

The following section presents the programming environment and the editing programs. First, each written program participating in the station's work was described. Next, the principles of code writing, its operation, and the entire process of controlling the station elements were discussed. Finally, the way of monitoring the station from anywhere in the world was described.

The other section describes the power and communication wiring of the station. The next part describes the operating voltages of the station components and the devices that are their sources.

The last chapter contains conclusions related to the station's construction and proposed improvements and adjustments that can be made in the future. Possible directions of development and a plan for further expansion were fleshed out. Solutions that the station can be equipped with were also discussed.
